What it is

A structured, print-and-fill record system for a single household. You complete it once, then keep it current with one pass a year. It is organized so that someone who had no part in creating it — an adjuster, an appraiser, a buyer, a family member — can still find what they need in it.

The kit starts with a 60-minute baseline: fill in the property profile, walk the house with the shot list, and log the four systems that matter most. Everything after that is added over time rather than finished in one sitting.

What’s inside

  1. Property Profile

    The one-page identity of your home. Most of this comes straight from your closing documents, your policy declarations page, and the county assessor’s record.

  2. Photo & Video Baseline

    Photographs are the backbone of the record. The master shot list, the walkthrough script, and the storage rules that keep ten years of photos findable.

  3. Master Home Inventory

    Room by room, everything of value: what it is, what identifies it, what it is worth, and whether a photo of it exists. One register page per room, with room value subtotals.

  4. Major Systems Register

    The big things that age, fail, and cost real money. Roof, HVAC, water heater, plumbing, electrical — with install dates, installers, condition notes, and expected replacement windows.

  5. Appliance & Serial Log

    One table for every data plate in the house. When a recall notice, warranty claim, or theft report asks for the model and serial, the answer is on this page.

  6. Receipts & Purchases

    Where the proof of purchase lives. Log significant purchases and note where the receipt itself is — paper file, email, or photo.

  7. Warranty Tracker

    Every warranty you own, when it dies, and who answers the phone. Item, provider, coverage type, start and expiration dates, registration confirmed.

  8. Repair & Service History

    The running log of everything fixed, serviced, or inspected — the single most valuable page when a problem turns out to be a repeat problem. Works for DIY and hired work alike.

  9. Improvements & Renovations

    Project-level records: what the project was, what it truly cost, who did it, and the paper that proves it. One card per project, with permits, dates, and warranty on the work.

  10. Important Documents

    The papers that define the property — and, critically, where each one physically or digitally lives. A location column for every item.

  11. Contractors & Services

    The directory of everyone who works on the house, so the next call, quote, or reference check starts from your own notes.

  12. Annual Review

    One pass a year keeps the whole record alive. The annual checklist, a five-year review log, and a record of what changed this year.

Where the record should live

A completed kit is valuable, which means it needs the same protection as the things it documents. Keep the working copy somewhere you will actually use it. Scan every completed page once a year into the same folder as your photos. Keep one full digital copy away from the property.

What this kit is not

This kit is a documentation and organization tool for your own records. It is not legal or insurance advice, does not interpret any policy or contract, and does not determine coverage, fault, or liability. It preserves facts. Decisions about any claim, dispute, or repair belong with you and the licensed professionals you choose.
