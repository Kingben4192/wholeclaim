# Project, Contract & Compliance Record Kit — Outline v0.2 (DRAFT)

**Kit 5 of 5**, Government Property & Records Management Kits line.
**Status:** Outline for founder review. No content written, no layout, no visual pass.
**Authorization:** Decisions #111 and #112 — Stage 1 (Build) only. Stage 2 (Validate) and Stage 3 (Scale) not authorized.

**v0.2 change:** capacity corrected from 25 projects per binder to **one primary contract or project per binder** (Decision #112). The former per-project two-page spread is expanded into full sections. This is the first kit where the line's shared architecture is deliberately not applied identically.

---

## 0. Format decisions

**Shared with the line** (Decision #112): record-spread architecture, Kit 1 §1 boundary language, recordkeeping conventions, regulatory Posture 1 (structural alignment, no printed citations, no conformance claim), restrained institutional visual direction — Pine / Ink / Paper, Stamp Red reserved for compliance-exception and overdue-deliverable flags, Brass Amber dropped.

**Decided for this kit specifically:**

- **Capacity: one primary contract or project per binder.** A COR or project manager administers a small number of instruments in depth, not many shallowly. The 25-unit default inherited during Stage 1 inverted the actual use case — it produced a shallow register where the buyer needs a deep file. Multi-contract portfolios use one binder per instrument.
- **Kit-specific boundary addition:** the kit does not interpret contract terms, determine compliance, or advise on contractual rights or remedies. It records what the user determines and when. Reading a clause and concluding what it requires is the user's judgment or their counsel's, never the kit's.

**Scope note:** property acquired under the contract is recorded in Kit 1; facility modifications delivered by it cross-reference Kit 2 §11; contract-related incidents route to Kit 4.

---

## 1. Product definition

**Buyer:** contracting officer's representative (COR), project manager, or program administrative officer responsible for administering a single contract or project.

**Job it does:** assemble a closeout-ready, audit-ready file for one instrument as it runs — milestones, deliverables, approvals, changes, inspections, correspondence — so the record is complete at closeout rather than reconstructed afterward.

## 2. Section structure

### Front Matter — ~4 pp.
Title page · What this kit is and is not (including the interpretation boundary above) · How to use this kit · Recordkeeping conventions · **Closeout-readiness note**: the file is built to be complete at any moment, not assembled at the end.

### Section 1 — Organization & Accountability Profile — ~2 pp.
Agency/component · Sub-office or program · Responsible officer (name, title, contact) · Role (COR / PM / administrative officer) · Designation reference and date · Alternate · Supervisor of record · Fiscal year · Record period covered · Binder serial and volume.

### Section 2 — Contract Summary & File Index — ~4 pp.
Replaces the former 25-row register. A one-instrument summary plus a navigation index to every section, so a reviewer handed the binder can orient in a minute. Contract or task order number · Title · Contractor and UEI · Type · Award date · Current period of performance · Current total value · Current obligated amount · Status · Section-by-section index with page references · Volume map where the file exceeds one binder.

### Section 3 — Contract Identification & Scope — ~4 pp.
Contract number and current modification level · Title · Contractor name, UEI, address, point of contact · Contract type (construction / services / supply / IDIQ task / interagency agreement / grant) · Award date · Notice to proceed or effective date · Original period of performance · Current period of performance · Original value · Current value · Funding source and appropriation · Scope summary as written in the instrument · Key personnel designated by the contractor · Government-furnished property (Kit 1 asset ID cross-references) · Place of performance · Prior related instruments.

### Section 4 — Milestone & Schedule Record — ~10 pp.
Milestone ID · Description · Baseline date · Revised date · Revision authority and modification reference · Actual completion date · Variance in days · Reason for variance as recorded by the user · Predecessor dependency · Impact on subsequent milestones · Status · Verified by and date. Expanded from the v0.1 spread block to carry a full schedule history rather than a summary.

### Section 5 — Deliverable & Acceptance Record — ~12 pp.
Deliverable ID · Description · Contract reference (CLIN or section) · Due date · Date submitted · Submitted by · Received by · Review period · Reviewed by · Review outcome (accepted / rejected / accepted with comment) · Comments recorded · Acceptance date · Accepting official signature · Rejection basis as recorded · Resubmission due date · Resubmission date · Final disposition · Document reference · Associated invoice reference.

### Section 6 — Financial & Invoice Record — ~12 pp.
Invoice number · Invoice date · Period covered · Amount invoiced · Deliverable or milestone referenced · Date received · Reviewed by · Review outcome · Approved amount · Withheld amount and basis · Retainage held · Approval date · Approving official signature · Payment date · Payment reference · Cumulative invoiced · Cumulative approved · Cumulative paid · Remaining obligated balance · Funding line · Fiscal year. All figures user-entered; the kit computes nothing.

### Section 7 — Approval & Authorization Log — ~8 pp.
Approval ID · Date requested · Requested by · Subject of approval · Authority basis as identified by the user · Approving official (name, title) · Decision (approved / denied / approved with condition) · Condition or limitation · Decision date · Signature · Document reference · Effect on scope, schedule, or cost · Related modification reference.

### Section 8 — Change Order & Modification Record — ~12 pp.
Modification number · Date issued · Type (administrative / bilateral / unilateral / change order / option exercise / extension / descope / termination) · Description of change · Originating request and requester · Justification as recorded · Effect on scope · Effect on schedule (days) · Effect on cost (amount) · Revised contract value · Revised period of performance · Negotiated by · Negotiation record reference · Approving official and signature · Contractor acceptance signature and date · Effective date · Downstream sections updated (checklist).

### Section 9 — Inspection & Quality Record — ~10 pp.
Inspection ID · Date · Type (progress / milestone / acceptance / punch list / warranty / regulatory) · Inspector name and organization · Location or work area · Scope inspected · Standard or specification referenced by the user · Result (accepted / rejected / conditional) · Deficiencies identified · Deficiency correction due date · Recheck date and result · Kit 2 deficiency cross-reference · Inspector signature · Contractor acknowledgment signature.

### Section 10 — Correspondence Log — ~12 pp.
Date · Direction (sent / received) · Party (name, organization, role) · Method (email / letter / portal / meeting / phone) · Subject · Summary of content · Contractual significance (Y/N) · Action required · Action taken and date · Response reference · Document storage reference · Recorded by. Sized for a full instrument history — correspondence volume is the most common reason a contract file fails an audit.

### Section 11 — Compliance & Requirement Tracking — ~12 pp.
User-populated; the kit supplies the structure, not the requirements. Requirement ID · Requirement description as entered by the user · Source document and reference as identified by the user · Applicable to (whole contract / specific deliverable / specific period) · Evidence of satisfaction · Evidence document reference · Verified by · Verification date · Status (satisfied / outstanding / not applicable / exception) · Exception basis and approval reference · Recurring (Y/N) and interval · Next verification due · Escalation reference where outstanding.

### Section 12 — Photographic Record — ~8 pp.
Photo ID · Date · Photographer · Subject (progress / condition / deficiency / completion / delivered item) · Location · Milestone or inspection reference · Notes. Mounting pages with fixed photo wells and caption blocks.

### Section 13 — Closeout Record — ~10 pp.
The section that makes the kit worth buying. Final deliverable accepted (date, by whom) · Final inspection completed and result · Punch list closed (date) · All modifications executed and reconciled · Final invoice received, approved, paid · Retainage released (date, amount) · Property accountability reconciled (Kit 1 cross-reference) · Warranty documentation received with start and end dates · As-built or final documentation received · Contractor release of claims executed (date, reference) · Government release executed · Excess funds deobligated (amount, date) · Contractor performance evaluation submitted (date, reference) · Subcontractor closeout confirmed · File retention determination and disposal-eligible date · Closeout certification signature and date.

### Section 14 — Audit Trail & Certification — ~6 pp.
Review date · Reviewer and organization · Review type (internal / IG / GAO / contract audit / financial / legal) · Scope and period · Documents provided · Findings · Corrective action and due date · Closure date · Signature. Periodic certification statement · Responsible officer signature and date · Supervisor signature and date · Period covered.

### Appendices A–F — ~14 pp.
A. Contract type definitions · B. Modification type definitions · C. Closeout checklist (extracted from §13 as a working one-pager) · D. Retention schedule worksheet · E. Glossary and abbreviations · F. Blank continuation sheets for §4, §5, §6, §8, §10, §11.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Contract size is the remaining capacity variable.** One binder holds one instrument, but a $50k service contract and a multi-year construction contract are not the same document. §10 and §11 are where a large instrument overflows first. A volume-map convention is sketched in §2; whether that's sufficient, or whether the kit needs a stated size ceiling, is unresolved.
2. **The no-citations posture costs the most in this kit.** A contract-administration buyer expects FAR-anchored vocabulary, and a kit that carefully names no authority may read as evasive rather than careful. §11 is user-populated precisely to sidestep this, which also means the kit supplies less than the buyer might expect. Posture 1 is line-wide, but per Decision #112 that is a shared decision, not an unrevisitable one — this is the kit with the strongest case for revisiting it.
3. **§13 Closeout may be the strongest standalone in the line**, alongside Kit 2's emergency section. Closeout is a genuine, recurring, widely-disliked chore with a clear completion state.
4. **Overlap with agency systems is highest here.** Contract writing systems already exist in most agencies. The paper buyer is the COR keeping a personal working file — a real person, but narrower than the kit implies. This is the kit whose buyer most needs confirming during Stage 2.
