## 0. Format decisions

- **Capacity:** 25 maintained equipment items per binder — decided for this kit, not inherited (Decision #112). Justification: sized for the small-shop and field-site buyer identified in §1, **not** for a large maintenance organization, which would run hundreds of items and belongs in a CMMS rather than a binder. This capacity is a deliberate narrowing to the buyer this kit can actually serve. Continuation packs extend it.
- **Regulatory posture:** structural alignment, no citations.
- **Visual system:** Pine / Ink / Paper. Stamp Red retained only for overdue-PM and safety-critical flags. Brass Amber dropped.

**Boundary language** — Kit 1 §1 front matter applies verbatim: not an official form, not a compliance certification, not a substitute for agency policy, not legal or audit advice. One addition specific to this kit: **the kit does not specify maintenance intervals or establish what maintenance is required** — the user records their own program against manufacturer and agency requirements. Setting intervals would be engineering guidance, which this line does not give.

**Scope note:** this kit records maintenance *execution*. The asset itself is described in Kit 1, the facility system in Kit 2. Both are cross-referenced, not duplicated.

## 1. Product definition

**Buyer:** maintenance supervisor, shop foreman, or facility operations officer running a preventive and corrective maintenance program on government equipment.

**Job it does:** prove that scheduled maintenance was performed, on what, by whom, when, at what cost — the record a reviewer asks for when equipment fails or a warranty claim is contested.

## 2. Section structure

### Front Matter — ~4 pp.
Title page · What this kit is and is not (including the interval-setting boundary above) · How to use this kit · Recordkeeping conventions.

### Section 1 — Organization & Accountability Profile — ~2 pp.
Agency/component · Sub-office or shop · Maintenance supervisor (name, title, contact) · Appointment date and delegation reference · Alternate · Supervisor of record · Fiscal year · Record period covered · Binder serial and volume.

### Section 2 — Equipment Register — ~8 pp.
Landscape index, 4 rows per page. Equipment ID · Nomenclature · Manufacturer · Model · Serial number · Kit 1 asset ID cross-reference · Kit 2 system ID cross-reference · Location · Installation or in-service date · Criticality (mission-critical / essential / routine) · PM program assigned (Y/N) · Current status (in service / down / awaiting parts / retired) · Detail-spread page reference.

### Section 3 — Preventive Maintenance Schedule Master — ~8 pp.
Program-level view across all equipment. Equipment ID · PM task ID · Task description · Interval basis (calendar / runtime hours / cycles / condition) · Interval value · Last performed date · Next due date · Assigned to (in-house / vendor) · Estimated labor hours · Required parts reference · Safety requirements or lockout/tagout needed (Y/N) · Status (current / due / overdue).

### Sections 4–7 — Per-Equipment Record Spread — ~50 pp. (2 pp. × 25)
One two-page opening per equipment item.

**4. Equipment Identification & Service Specification** — Equipment ID · Nomenclature, manufacturer, model, serial · Kit 1 / Kit 2 cross-references · Location · In-service date · Manufacturer service manual reference · Lubricant/consumable specifications · Operating parameters · Criticality rating · Warranty status and expiration · Service contract reference.

**5. Preventive Maintenance Completion Log** — Date performed · PM task ID · Interval basis reading (date / hours / cycles at service) · Performed by (name, in-house or vendor) · Tasks completed · Parts consumed · Labor hours · Findings or abnormal conditions · Deficiency raised (Y/N) and reference · Next due · Technician signature · Supervisor verification.

**6. Repair & Corrective Maintenance History** — Date reported · Date started · Date completed · Work order number · Failure description · Symptom · Cause determined · Corrective action performed · Parts replaced · Labor hours · Downtime hours · Cost (parts / labor / total) · Performed by · Return-to-service verification and signature.

**7. Parts & Consumables Record** — Date · Part number · Description · Quantity · Unit cost · Total cost · Source or vendor · Purchase or stock reference · Installed on (work order or PM reference) · Warranty on part · Removed part disposition.

### Section 8 — Work Order Log — ~10 pp.
Master chronological log across all equipment. Work order number · Date opened · Priority (emergency / urgent / routine / deferred) · Requested by · Equipment ID · Location · Description of work requested · Type (PM / corrective / modification / inspection) · Assigned to · Date scheduled · Date completed · Labor hours · Total cost · Status · Closed by and date.

### Section 9 — Inspection & Testing Record — ~8 pp.
Distinct from PM: verification rather than service. Date · Equipment ID · Inspection type (safety / regulatory / operational / acceptance / post-repair / calibration) · Inspector name and organization · Standard or procedure referenced · Instrument used and calibration date · Result (pass / fail / conditional) · Readings or measurements · Deficiencies identified · Next inspection due · Inspector signature.

### Section 10 — Vendor & Service Provider Register — ~6 pp.
Vendor name · Vendor ID or DUNS/UEI · Services provided · Contract or BPA number · Contract period · Point of contact (name, phone, email) · After-hours contact · Response-time commitment · Rate structure · Insurance/bonding status and expiration · Equipment covered · Performance notes · Active status.

### Section 11 — Cost & Labor Record — ~6 pp.
Period (month/quarter) · Equipment ID or cost center · In-house labor hours · In-house labor cost · Contract labor cost · Parts cost · Materials cost · Total maintenance cost · Funding source · Budget line · Variance from planned · Cumulative fiscal-year total.

### Section 12 — Photographic Record — ~8 pp.
Photo ID · Equipment ID · Date · Photographer · Subject (pre-repair condition / failure / post-repair / part / nameplate) · Associated work order · Notes. Mounting pages with photo wells and caption blocks.

### Section 13 — Supporting Documentation Index — ~6 pp.
Document number · Type (service manual / PM procedure / work order / invoice / parts receipt / test certificate / calibration certificate / warranty / vendor contract / correspondence / photograph) · Date · Source · Storage location · Retention period · Disposal-eligible date · Associated equipment ID.

### Section 14 — Audit, Review & Certification — ~4 pp.
Review date · Reviewer and organization · Review type (internal / IG / external audit / warranty / insurance) · Scope · Findings · Corrective action and due date · Closure date · Signature. Periodic certification statement · Maintenance supervisor signature and date · Supervisor signature and date · Period covered.

### Appendices A–F — ~14 pp.
A. Priority and criticality definitions · B. Interval basis reference (calendar / runtime / cycles / condition) · C. Failure cause category list · D. Retention schedule worksheet · E. Glossary and abbreviations · F. Blank continuation sheets for §2, §3, §8.

## 5. Open questions

1. **This is the kit most likely to lose to software.** CMMS/EAM systems own this workflow in any agency large enough to have one. The paper buyer is the small shop, the field site, or the environment where a system exists but isn't trusted. Worth confirming that buyer is real before this kit is built out.
2. **Interval-setting boundary** — confirm the front-matter language. Recording maintenance is safe; specifying what maintenance is required is engineering guidance and I've drawn the line at recording only.
3. **§8 Work Order Log duplicates data** already in §5 and §6 by design — the log is chronological across equipment, the spreads are per-equipment. That double entry is defensible on paper but is the kind of thing a real user resents. Worth testing.
4. **Kit 2 seam** at inspections and deficiencies — same flag as Kit 2 §10. A buyer owning both needs an obvious rule for which binder a work order lives in.
