## 0. Format decisions

- **Capacity:** 25 incidents per binder — decided for this kit, not inherited (Decision #112). Justification: for most offices 25 incidents represents several years of records, so the binder spans a realistic retention period rather than a reporting cycle. Continuation packs extend it.
- **Regulatory posture:** structural alignment, no printed citations.
- **Visual system:** restrained institutional direction — Pine / Ink / Paper. Stamp Red is functionally correct throughout this kit and is used more here than anywhere else in the line. Brass Amber dropped.

**Boundary language** — Kit 1 §1 front matter applies verbatim, with two additions specific to this kit, both of which I'd treat as non-negotiable:

- **The kit does not assign fault, liability, or culpability.** It records what was observed, when, by whom. Determination of liability is the agency's, through its own process.
- **The kit does not value a loss or estimate a claim.** The user enters their own figures from their own sources. This is the same boundary the software already holds — no valuation, no outcome prediction.

This is the highest-risk kit in the line. An incident record is the document most likely to end up in an investigation, a claim, or litigation, and the one where a badly worded prompt could push a user toward writing something prejudicial about themselves or a colleague. Field wording here needs more care than anywhere else in the line, and I'd want that flagged before content is written.

**Scope note:** affected property is identified here but described in Kit 1; damaged building systems are cross-referenced to Kit 2; repair work is executed and recorded in Kit 3.

## 1. Product definition

**Buyer:** property custodian, safety officer, security officer, or administrative officer responsible for documenting incidents affecting government property.

**Job it does:** capture an incident completely and contemporaneously — sequence, damage, evidence, notifications, corrective action — in the form that survives scrutiny months later when memory has degraded and the file is all that remains.

## 2. Section structure

### Front Matter — ~4 pp.
Title page · What this kit is and is not (including both boundary additions above) · How to use this kit · **Contemporaneous recording guidance**: record what was observed, distinguish observation from inference, do not speculate on cause in the initial report, correct by single-line strike with initial and date, never erase or obliterate · Recordkeeping conventions.

### Section 1 — Organization & Accountability Profile — ~2 pp.
Agency/component · Sub-office · Responsible officer (name, title, contact) · Appointment date and delegation reference · Alternate · Supervisor of record · Fiscal year · Record period covered · Binder serial and volume.

### Section 2 — Incident Register — ~8 pp.
Landscape index, 4 rows per page. Incident ID · Date of occurrence · Date discovered · Incident type (damage / loss / theft / destruction / vandalism / fire / water / weather / vehicle / injury-involving / other) · Brief description · Location · Affected property reference · Estimated value · Reported to (internal / police / insurer) · Investigation status · Resolution status · Detail-spread page reference.

### Sections 3–6 — Per-Incident Record Spread — ~50 pp. (2 pp. × 25)
One two-page opening per incident.

**3. Incident Identification & Initial Report** — Incident ID · Date and time of occurrence · Date and time discovered · Discovered by (name, title) · Reported by · Date and time reported · Location (building, room, Kit 2 space ID) · Incident type · Weather or environmental conditions if relevant · Initial description as observed · Persons present · Reporting official signature and date.

**4. Timeline & Sequence of Events** — Chronological entry rows: date · time · event or observation · source (direct observation / report / record) · recorded by. Designed for entries to be added as facts emerge, each independently dated and attributed rather than rewritten into a narrative.

**5. Affected Property & Damage Assessment** — Item ID · Kit 1 asset ID cross-reference · Kit 2 system ID cross-reference · Description · Serial number · Location at time of incident · Damage classification (destroyed / major / minor / missing / undamaged) · Damage description · Operational status after incident · Assessed by (name, title) · Assessment date · Repair or replacement determination · Cost figure entered by user and its source.

**6. Immediate Actions & Notifications** — Action taken · Date and time · Taken by · Scene secured (Y/N, by whom, time) · Property secured or relocated · Hazard mitigated · Notifications made: recipient, role, method, date/time, acknowledged by · Police or security report number · Insurer notified (Y/N, reference) · Higher headquarters notified.

### Section 7 — Damage Documentation Detail — ~8 pp.
Expanded per-item documentation where the spread's §5 block is insufficient. Item ID · Detailed damage description by component · Measurements or extent · Pre-incident condition and its evidentiary source · Post-incident condition · Contributing environmental factors observed · Salvage or recovery potential · Disposition determination · Kit 1 disposition cross-reference · Assessor signature.

### Section 8 — Photographic & Evidence Record — ~12 pp.
The largest photographic section in the line, deliberately. Photo or evidence ID · Incident ID · Date and time taken · Photographer or collector · Location and view/angle · Subject depicted · Chain-of-custody entries (released by, received by, date/time, purpose) · Storage location · Notes. Mounting pages with fixed photo wells and caption blocks beneath.

### Section 9 — Witness & Statement Record — ~6 pp.
Witness name · Title and organization · Contact information · Relationship to incident · Date and time of statement · Taken by · Location taken · Statement (ruled space, written in witness's own words) · Witness signature and date · Recording official signature. Includes the instruction that statements are recorded as given and not summarized or characterized by the recorder.

### Section 10 — Investigation & Cause Determination — ~8 pp.
Investigation reference number · Date opened · Investigating official or board · Authority or appointment reference · Scope · Evidence reviewed · Findings of fact · Cause determination · Contributing factors · Date closed · Report reference · Financial liability determination reference · Relief from accountability granted (Y/N) · Kit 1 LDDT cross-reference · Approving official signature.

### Section 11 — Corrective & Preventive Action Log — ~8 pp.
Action ID · Incident ID · Finding addressed · Action type (corrective / preventive) · Description · Assigned to · Assigned date · Target completion · Actual completion · Verified by and date · Effectiveness review date and result · Kit 3 work-order cross-reference · Status.

### Section 12 — Cost & Financial Impact Record — ~6 pp.
All figures user-entered with source recorded; the kit computes nothing. Cost category (repair / replacement / labor / disposal / temporary measures / other) · Description · Amount · Source of figure (estimate / invoice / appraisal / catalog) · Document reference · Date · Funding source · Recovered amount (insurance / restitution / salvage) · Net loss · Fiscal year · Certifying official.

### Section 13 — Communications & Notification Log — ~6 pp.
Date and time · Direction (sent / received) · Party (name, organization, role) · Method (email / phone / letter / in person) · Subject · Summary of content · Action required · Action taken · Document reference · Recorded by.

### Section 14 — Audit, Review & Certification — ~4 pp.
Review date · Reviewer and organization · Review type (internal / IG / legal / insurance / external audit) · Scope · Findings · Corrective action and due date · Closure date · Signature. Case closure certification · Responsible officer signature and date · Supervisor signature and date · Period covered.

### Appendices A–F — ~14 pp.
A. Incident type definitions · B. Damage classification definitions · C. Notification requirement worksheet (agency-completed, not prescribed by the kit) · D. Retention schedule worksheet · E. Glossary and abbreviations · F. Blank continuation sheets for §2, §4, §8, §9.

## 5. Open questions

1. **This kit needs a legal read before content, not after.** It is the one most likely to surface in litigation or an investigation, and field wording can materially affect what a user writes about themselves or a colleague. I'd gate content on that read rather than draft and revise.
2. **Chain of custody in §8 is a real commitment.** Including those fields implies the kit supports evidentiary handling. If we're not prepared to stand behind that, the fields should be softened to a simple access log instead.
3. **§9 witness statements may be a step too far.** Statement-taking is a regulated activity in many agency contexts. Consider whether the kit should provide a statement form at all, or only an index pointing to statements taken through the agency's own process.
4. **Fault-avoidance holds only if the field wording holds.** The §10 cause-determination section sits closest to the line. It should record the agency's determination, never invite the user to reach one.
