## 1. Product definition

**Buyer:** property custodian, accountable property officer, or administrative officer inside a federal, state, or local agency or a contractor holding government-furnished property.

**Job it does:** maintain a defensible, self-contained paper record of accountable property from acquisition through disposition, in a form that survives custodian turnover and can be handed to a reviewer intact.

**Register:** institutional, formal, records-management. No warmth, no second-person coaching, no consumer reassurance language. Instructions read as procedure, not encouragement.

**What it is not** — and this belongs in printed front matter, not just in this file:

- Not an official government form, and not issued by or affiliated with any agency.
- Not a compliance certification. Using the kit does not establish that any statutory, regulatory, or agency requirement has been met.
- Not a substitute for the user's own agency property-management policy, which governs where the two differ.
- Not legal, audit, or accounting advice.

This mirrors the boundary the Product Bible already holds for the software (never advises, never predicts outcomes) and should be treated as the same class of invariant for this line.

## 2. `[RESOLVED]` Binder capacity — Option A, 25 assets (this kit only)

> **Scope of this decision, corrected per #112:** 25 assets is **Kit 1's** capacity, not a line-wide rule. Each kit sets its own from its own use case — Kit 5 was subsequently corrected to one contract per binder for exactly this reason. Read the table below as Kit 1 reasoning only.

The single biggest driver of page count and unit cost. Every field list below is capacity-neutral; only the page budget changes.

| Option | Assets covered | Est. printed pages | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — 25 assets** *(recommended)* | 25 | ~128 | Fits a 1" – 1.5" binder. Realistic for a single office or cost center. Continuation packs sold separately. |
| B — 50 assets | 50 | ~205 | 2" binder minimum. Heavier unit cost, narrower buyer. |
| C — Register-only, 100+ assets | 100+ | ~70 | Drops per-asset detail spreads entirely; becomes an index, not a record. Weakest audit story. |

**Recommendation: Option A.** It keeps the per-asset detail spread — which is the part that makes the kit defensible under review — and pushes scale to continuation packs, which is also the better repeat-revenue shape. Page counts below assume A.

## 3. `[RESOLVED]` Regulatory-alignment posture — Posture 1, no printed citations

The brief says "aligned to federal recordkeeping norms." There are two very different ways to build that, and they carry different risk.

- **Posture 1 — Structural alignment, no citations** *(recommended)*. Field names and workflow mirror standard federal property-management practice (NSN, IUID/UII, hand receipt, excess/surplus disposition, physical inventory reconciliation), but the kit cites no specific regulation and claims no conformance. Lowest risk; still recognizable to the buyer.
- **Posture 2 — Cited conformance.** The kit names FAR Part 45, 41 CFR 102-3x, MIL-STD-130, etc., and asserts alignment. Materially stronger sales claim, but every citation must be verified current before print, and the conformance claim itself likely needs counsel review — it is the kind of claim that turns a template into a represented compliance product.

**Recommendation: Posture 1 for v1.** I want to flag plainly that I have *not* verified any regulatory citation against current text, and would not put one in print without doing so. If you want Posture 2, that's a separate research-and-counsel pass before content, not something to fold into the writing.

## 4. `[RESOLVED]` Visual system — Pine/Ink/Paper, Brass Amber dropped

Your open question from the brief, unresolved on purpose. Two viable paths:

- **Keep the existing palette** (Pine `#1E4636`, Paper `#F2F0EB`, Stamp Red `#A32C1C`, Brass Amber `#8A6D2F`, Ink `#14231C`, per `01_Brand/Brand-Book-v1.1.md`). Pine and Ink already read as serious; Paper is genuinely good for form stock. Stamp Red and Brass Amber are the consumer-warm notes and would need to be demoted to rare accent or dropped.
- **Distinct institutional system.** Neutral greys, single restrained accent, heavier rule lines, denser type. Reads closer to a government form.

**My read:** Pine + Ink + Paper, with Stamp Red retained *only* for LDDT and discrepancy sections where alarm is functionally correct, and Brass Amber dropped from this line. That keeps one company visually while shedding the consumer warmth. But this is a brand call and it's yours — I'm not picking it.

## 5. Section structure

Fourteen sections plus front matter and appendices. Order follows asset lifecycle, which is also the order a reviewer traverses it.

### Front Matter — ~4 pp.
Title page · What this kit is and is not (§1 boundary language, printed in full) · How to use this kit · Recordkeeping conventions: permanent ink, single-line strike corrections with initial and date, no erasure or obliteration, date format, signature requirements, retention handling.

### Section 1 — Organization & Accountability Profile — ~2 pp.
Agency/component · Sub-office or cost center · Physical address · Accountable property officer (name, title, contact) · Appointment date and delegation reference · Alternate/deputy custodian · Supervisor of record · Fiscal year · Record period covered · Binder serial and volume number.

### Section 2 — Asset Identification Register — ~8 pp.
Master index; landscape, 4 rows per page. Every asset appears here once and points to its detail spread.

Local asset/tag ID · Nomenclature/description · Manufacturer · Model · Serial number · National Stock Number (NSN) · IUID/UII · Part number · Quantity and unit of issue · Condition code · Classification (accountable / capitalized / sensitive / expendable) · Detail-spread page reference · Current record status (active / transferred / disposed).

### Sections 3–6 — Per-Asset Record Spread — ~50 pp. (2 pp. × 25 assets)
The core of the kit. One two-page spread per asset, carrying four sub-records so an asset's whole life sits in one opening.

**3. Acquisition & Receipt** — Acquisition method (purchase / transfer-in / donation / fabrication / government-furnished) · Contract or PO number · Requisition number · Vendor · Acquisition date · Receipt date · Received by (name, signature) · Inspection and acceptance date · Accepted by · Acquisition cost · Funding source/appropriation · Capitalization threshold met (Y/N) · Useful life · Warranty start and end · Warranty document reference.

**4. Location & Assignment** — Building · Floor · Room or sub-location · Organization assigned · Assigned user or position · Assignment date · On-site / off-site · Off-site use authorization reference · Expected return date.

**5. Custody & Hand Receipt** — Custodian name, title, ID · Receipt date · Custodian signature · Printed acknowledgment-of-responsibility statement · Relief-of-custody date · Condition at return · Receiving official signature · Detachable duplicate hand receipt.

**6. Transfer** — Transfer type (internal / inter-agency / to contractor / external) · From organization · To organization · Authorization document number · Approving official · Transfer date · Shipment method and tracking · Condition at transfer · Receiving acknowledgment signature and date · Updated location reference.

### Section 7 — Physical Inventory & Reconciliation — ~8 pp.
Inventory date · Cycle type (annual / spot / change-of-custodian / closeout) · Inventory team · Verification method (sight verification / barcode / RFID) · Asset ID · Found / not found · Condition observed · Discrepancy noted (Y/N) · Discrepancy type (overage / shortage / mislocation / misidentification) · Reconciliation action taken · Resolution date · Certifying official signature and date.

### Section 8 — Condition, Maintenance & Status Change — ~6 pp.
Deliberately light. Deep maintenance belongs to Kit 3 and this section cross-references it rather than duplicating it.

Date · Event type (repair / upgrade / modification / downgrade / damage) · Condition code before and after · Description · Cost · Performed by (internal/vendor) · Kit 3 work-order reference · Return-to-service date.

### Section 9 — Loss, Damage, Destruction & Theft (LDDT) — ~6 pp.
Also deliberately light; Kit 4 carries full incident documentation.

Date of occurrence · Date discovered · Discovered by · Description and circumstances · Police or security report number · Estimated value of loss · Investigation reference · Financial liability determination or board-of-survey reference · Outcome · Relief from accountability granted (Y/N) · Kit 4 cross-reference.

### Section 10 — Disposition & Disposal — ~8 pp.
Determination date · Reason (excess / obsolete / beyond economical repair / end of useful life) · Disposition method (transfer / donation / sale / recycling / trade-in / abandonment or destruction) · Authorization document number · Approving official · Disposal date · Receiving entity · Proceeds, if any · Certificate of destruction reference · **Data sanitization performed (Y/N), method, certifying official** — non-optional for any IT or data-bearing asset · Final record closure signature.

### Section 11 — Supporting Documentation Index — ~6 pp.
Document number · Document type (PO / invoice / packing slip / receiving report / hand receipt / transfer document / inspection report / photograph / certificate of destruction / correspondence) · Document date · Source · Storage location (physical file / digital path) · Retention period · Disposal-eligible date · Associated asset ID.

### Section 12 — Photographic Record — ~10 pp.
Photo ID · Associated asset ID · Date taken · Photographer · View or angle · Condition depicted · Notes. Mounting pages with fixed photo wells and caption blocks beneath.

### Section 13 — Audit & Review Trail — ~4 pp.
Review date · Reviewer name and organization · Review type (internal / IG / GAO / external audit / change-of-custodian) · Scope · Findings · Corrective action · Corrective action due date · Closure date · Signature.

### Section 14 — Certification — ~2 pp.
Periodic certification statement · Accountable property officer signature and date · Supervisor signature and date · Certification period covered.

### Appendices A–F — ~14 pp.
A. Condition code reference · B. Classification definitions (accountable / capitalized / sensitive / expendable) · C. Disposition pathway glossary · D. Retention schedule worksheet · E. Glossary and abbreviations (NSN, IUID, UII, GFP, APO, LDDT, BER) · F. Blank continuation sheets for §2, §7, §11.
