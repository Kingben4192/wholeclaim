## 0. Format decisions

**Shared with the line** (#112, #113): record-spread architecture, boundary language, recordkeeping conventions, regulatory Posture 1 (structural alignment with federal recordkeeping practice, no printed citations, no conformance claim), restrained institutional visual direction — Pine / Ink / Paper, Stamp Red reserved for life-safety and condition-alarm use, Brass Amber dropped.

**Decided for this kit:**

- **Capacity:** one facility per binder — 25 interior spaces, 15 major systems. Justification: a mid-size government building carries roughly that many documented spaces and major systems, so one binder maps to one facility without continuation in the typical case. Campus and multi-building sites use one binder per building.

**Boundary language** (front matter, printed in full):

- Not an official government form; not issued by or affiliated with any agency.
- Not a compliance certification. Use of the kit does not establish that any statutory, regulatory, or agency requirement has been met.
- Not a substitute for agency policy, applicable law, procurement requirements, engineering inspections, safety inspections, environmental assessments, or professional evaluations.
- Not legal, audit, or accounting advice.

**Register note:** the supplied copy's voice ("Know what you own. Know what condition it is in.") is retained for product-page use. Binder front matter stays institutional.

**Scope note:** movable assets are recorded in the Asset & Inventory Kit; inspections, maintenance and deficiency tracking in the Maintenance & Inspection Kit; incidents in the Incident & Loss Documentation Kit; construction and renovation projects in the Project & Capital Improvement Kit.

## 1. Product definition

**Buyer:** facility manager, property administrator, or administrative officer responsible for a government-occupied building.

**Job it does:** establish and maintain a standing record of one facility — identification, profile, exterior and interior condition, major systems, improvements, and life-safety information — understandable by personnel who were not present when it was created.

**Documentation standard** (front matter): the goal is not to collect photographs and paperwork, but to produce a repeatable property record legible to someone who had no part in making it.

## 2. Section structure

### Front Matter — ~4 pp.
Title page · What this kit is and is not · How to use this kit · Recordkeeping conventions: permanent ink, single-line strike corrections initialed and dated, no erasure or obliteration, date format, signature requirements, retention handling · Documentation standard.

### Section 1 — Property Identification & Accountability — ~4 pp.
Facility name · Facility identification number · Physical location and address · Department or agency · Property classification · Responsible division · Primary contact (name, title, contact) · Alternate contact · Responsible officer designation reference and date · Supervisor of record · Date documented · Documentation period covered · Fiscal year · Binder serial and volume number.

### Section 2 — Facility Profile — ~4 pp.
Building type · Approximate age / year constructed · Construction characteristics · Gross square footage · Net usable square footage · Number of floors · Basement or sub-level (Y/N) · Major spaces · Occupancy classification · Design occupant load · Accessibility features · Site characteristics and acreage · Parking (type, capacity) · Exterior improvements · Ownership status (owned / leased / permit) and agreement reference · Historic designation status · Utility service accounts · Prior facility identifiers.

### Section 3 — Exterior Record — ~10 pp.
One block per element, each carrying: element · material or type · approximate age · observed condition · condition code · date observed · observed by · notes · photograph reference.

Elements: roof · exterior walls · windows · exterior doors · foundation · walkways · parking areas · drainage · landscaping · fencing · exterior lighting · signage · exterior structures (sheds, canopies, shelters) · loading areas · site utilities at grade.

### Section 4 — Interior Space Register — ~6 pp.
Landscape index, 4 rows per page. Space ID · Room name or function · Floor · Net square footage · Occupancy type · Assigned organization · Assigned occupant or position · Access restriction level · Condition code · Status (active / vacant / under renovation / closed) · Detail page reference.

### Section 5 — Interior Record — ~14 pp.
Per-space detail, two spaces per page across 25 spaces. Space ID · Room identification · Flooring (material, condition) · Walls (finish, condition) · Ceiling (type, condition) · Doors · Windows · Fixtures · Built-in equipment · Installed improvements · Observable conditions · Date observed · Observed by · Photograph references · Notes.

### Section 6 — Major Systems Register — ~6 pp.
Landscape index of the 15 systems carried in §7. System ID · System type (HVAC / electrical / plumbing / fire protection / security / communications / emergency systems / building controls / elevator / roof system / envelope / other installed) · Manufacturer · Model · Serial number · Installation date · Capacity or rating · Area served · Expected service life · Condition code · Detail page reference.

### Section 7 — Major Systems Record — ~16 pp.
Per-system detail, roughly one page per system across 15 systems. System ID · Type and subtype · Manufacturer, model, serial · Installation date · Installing contractor · Capacity or rating · Fuel or power source · Areas and spaces served · Design standard referenced by the user · Replacement cost estimate as entered by the user · Expected end of service life · **Warranty provider, warranty start and end, coverage scope, exclusions** · Service contract number, provider, contact, coverage period · Observed condition and date · Observed by · Photograph references.

> Warranty tracking is retained here deliberately. It was reduced to a closeout line item in the supplied copy; warranty expiry is a recurring and avoidable loss in facility management, and it belongs on the standing system record rather than in a project file.

### Section 8 — Installed Improvements & Grounds — ~6 pp.
Improvement ID · Description · Type (installed equipment / built-in improvement / site feature / grounds structure) · Location · Date installed · Installed by · Cost as entered by the user · Associated project reference (Project & Capital Improvement Kit) · Warranty reference · Condition · Photograph reference · Notes.

### Section 9 — Emergency Information & Life Safety — ~6 pp.
**Restored by explicit decision (#113)** after being dropped in the supplied copy. Flagged in the original Stage 1 outline as possibly the strongest standalone value in the line — the section most likely to be used daily rather than filed.

Emergency contact roster (name, role, 24-hour contact) · Utility shutoff locations with space references: water, gas, electrical, fire suppression · Fire alarm panel location · Sprinkler riser and fire department connection location · Emergency generator location, fuel type, capacity, test schedule and last test · Evacuation assembly points · Occupant emergency plan reference and revision date · Last drill date and type · AED and first-aid locations · Hazardous material storage locations and inventory reference · Accessibility egress provisions · Local responding fire and police jurisdiction and contact · Key or access control custodian.

### Section 10 — Property Change Record — ~6 pp.
Date · Change type (repair / improvement / alteration / replacement / removal / reconfiguration / occupancy change) · Description · Location or space ID · Associated system ID · Authorization reference · Approving official · Performed by · Cost as entered by the user · Related project reference · Permit reference · As-built documentation reference · Effect on facility profile fields · Recorded by and date.

### Section 11 — Photographic Documentation — ~12 pp.
Each photograph associated with: photo reference number · location or space ID · area · associated asset or system ID · date taken · photographer · description · condition depicted · notes. Mounting pages with fixed photo wells and caption blocks beneath.

### Section 12 — Supporting Documentation Index — ~6 pp.
Document number · Type (lease or agreement / drawing / as-built / permit / warranty / service contract / correspondence / photograph / emergency plan / prior report) · Document date · Source · Storage location (physical file / digital path) · Retention period · Disposal-eligible date · Associated space or system ID.

### Section 13 — Audit, Review & Certification — ~4 pp.
Review date · Reviewer name and organization · Review type (internal / IG / external audit / insurance / change-of-manager) · Scope · Findings · Corrective action and due date · Closure date · Signature. Periodic certification statement · Responsible officer signature and date · Supervisor signature and date · Period covered.

### Appendices A–F — ~12 pp.
A. Condition code reference · B. System type definitions · C. Space classification definitions · D. Retention schedule worksheet · E. Glossary and abbreviations · F. Blank continuation sheets for §4, §5, §10.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Settled: this kit is the Stage 2 validation lead** (#111, amended). Chosen as the broadest foundational layer — it tests the core format, workflow and evidence structure itself rather than one specialized use case, and its results inform the other four kits without premature specialization. **What follows from that:** validation should probe the *shared* architecture, not this kit's facility specifics — whether the register → detail-record → photographic → certification pattern survives contact with a real user, whether the recordkeeping conventions in front matter are followed or ignored, and whether one binder per facility is the right unit. Findings on those points transfer to all five kits; findings about exterior element lists do not. Stage 2 itself remains unauthorized.
2. **Exterior and interior condition records (§3, §5) overlap the Maintenance & Inspection Kit's inspection sections.** The intended rule is that this kit records *standing condition* and that kit records *dated inspection events*. That distinction is clear on paper and routinely collapses in practice.
3. **15 major systems may be low** for a large or technically complex facility. Continuation sheets cover it, but the register in §6 is sized to 15.
4. **§9 may deserve to be its own product.** Same flag as the original Stage 1 outline; restoring it here does not settle that.
