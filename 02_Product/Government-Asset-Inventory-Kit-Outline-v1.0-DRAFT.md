## 0. Format decisions

**Shared with the line** (#112, #113): record-spread architecture, boundary language, recordkeeping conventions, regulatory Posture 1, restrained institutional visual direction — Pine / Ink / Paper, Stamp Red reserved for discrepancy and loss flags, Brass Amber dropped.

**Decided for this kit:**

- **Capacity:** 25 assets per binder. Justification: keeps the per-asset detail spread, which is what makes the record defensible under review; scale is handled by continuation packs rather than by thinning each record. This capacity was approved for this kit specifically and is not a line-wide rule (#112).

**Accountability spine — restored (#113).** The supplied copy's asset kit kept identification, location, condition, and a change log, and dropped acquisition and receipt, custody, transfer authorization, inventory reconciliation, loss/damage/theft, and disposition. Those are restored here deliberately.

> **Why.** An inventory list answers *what do we have*. An accountable property record answers *who was responsible, on what authority, and where did it go* — which is the question a property review actually asks. Two items carry outsized consequence: the **custody hand-receipt signature**, which is what makes the record evidentiary rather than administrative; and **data sanitization certification on disposal**, without which a data-bearing government asset leaving custody is a breach exposure. Neither is optional in this kit.

**Boundary language** (front matter, printed in full): not an official government form · not a compliance certification · not a substitute for agency policy, applicable law, or procurement requirements · not legal, audit, or accounting advice.

**Scope note:** the facility and its fixed systems are recorded in the Property Documentation Kit; maintenance in the Maintenance & Inspection Kit; incidents in the Incident & Loss Documentation Kit.

## 1. Product definition

**Buyer:** property custodian, accountable property officer, or administrative officer responsible for movable government property — including a contractor holding government-furnished property.

**Job it does:** maintain a defensible record of accountable property from acquisition through disposition, in a form that survives custodian turnover and can be handed to a reviewer intact.

**Core principle** (front matter): every important physical asset should have an identifiable record. Without consistent documentation, institutional knowledge disappears when personnel change.

## 2. Section structure

### Front Matter — ~4 pp.
Title page · What this kit is and is not · How to use this kit · Recordkeeping conventions · Core principle.

### Section 1 — Organization & Accountability Profile — ~2 pp.
Agency or department · Sub-office, division, or responsible unit · Accountable property officer (name, title, contact) · Designation reference and date · Alternate or deputy custodian · Supervisor of record · Fiscal year · Record period covered · Binder serial and volume number.

### Section 2 — Asset Identification Register — ~8 pp.
Landscape index, 4 rows per page; every asset appears once and points to its detail spread. Local asset identification number · Asset name and description · Manufacturer · Model · Serial number · **National Stock Number (NSN)** · **IUID / UII** · Part number · Category · Quantity and unit of issue · Condition classification · Accountability classification (accountable / capitalized / sensitive / expendable) · Location · Responsible unit · Detail-spread page reference · Record status (active / transferred / disposed).

**Asset categories** (appendix-defined, register-selected): office equipment · furniture · appliances · tools · machinery · maintenance equipment · technology equipment · communications equipment · facility equipment · specialized equipment · vehicles · storage inventory · other physical property.

### Sections 3–6 — Per-Asset Record Spread — ~50 pp. (2 pp. × 25)
One two-page opening per asset, carrying four sub-records so an asset's full standing is visible in a single view.

**3. Asset Identification & Specification** — Asset ID · Name and description · Manufacturer, model, serial · NSN · IUID/UII · Part number · Category · Accountability classification · Quantity and unit of issue · Configuration or installed position · Nameplate data · Photograph references.

**4. Acquisition & Receipt** — *(restored)* Acquisition method (purchase / transfer-in / donation / fabrication / government-furnished) · Contract or purchase order number · Requisition number · Vendor · Acquisition date · Receipt date · Received by (name, signature) · Inspection and acceptance date · Accepted by · Acquisition cost as entered by the user · Funding source or appropriation · Capitalization threshold met (Y/N) · Date placed in service · Useful life · Warranty start and end · Warranty document reference.

**5. Location & Assignment** — Building · Floor · Room or area · Storage location · Department · Responsible unit · Assigned user or position · Assignment date · On-site / off-site · Off-site use authorization reference · Expected return date.

**6. Custody & Hand Receipt** — *(restored)* Custodian name, title, identifier · Receipt date · **Custodian signature** · Printed acknowledgment-of-responsibility statement · Relief-of-custody date · Condition at return · Receiving official signature · Detachable duplicate hand receipt.

### Section 7 — Transfer Record — ~8 pp.
*(restored)* Transfer type (internal / inter-department / inter-agency / to contractor / external) · From organization · To organization · **Authorization document number** · **Approving official and signature** · Transfer date · Shipment method and tracking · Condition at transfer · Receiving acknowledgment signature and date · Updated location and responsible unit · Register updated (Y/N, by whom).

### Section 8 — Asset Change Log — ~6 pp.
Adopted from the supplied copy. Date · Asset ID · Change type (relocated / reassigned / repaired / modified / replaced / removed / retired / transferred / surplused) · Description · Authorization reference · Performed or authorized by · Prior value · New value · Supporting document reference · Recorded by.

### Section 9 — Inventory Review & Reconciliation — ~8 pp.
*(restored, merged with the copy's inventory review fields)* Review date · Review type (annual / spot / change-of-custodian / closeout) · Reviewer or inventory team · Verification method (sight verification / barcode scan / RFID) · Asset ID · Asset status · Location verification (verified / not at recorded location / not found) · Condition observed · Discrepancy noted (Y/N) · Discrepancy type (overage / shortage / mislocation / misidentification) · Corrective action · Follow-up notes · Resolution date · **Certifying official signature and date**.

### Section 10 — Condition & Status Record — ~6 pp.
Date · Asset ID · Condition classification before and after (new / good / fair / poor / damaged / out of service / pending repair / pending replacement / surplus / other agency-defined) · Event type (inspection / repair / upgrade / modification / downgrade / damage) · Description · Cost as entered by the user · Performed by · Maintenance & Inspection Kit work-order cross-reference · Return-to-service date.

### Section 11 — Loss, Damage, Destruction & Theft — ~6 pp.
*(restored; deliberately light — full incident documentation belongs to the Incident & Loss Documentation Kit)* Date of occurrence · Date discovered · Discovered by · Asset ID · Description and circumstances · Police or security report number · Estimated value as entered by the user · Investigation reference · Financial liability determination or board-of-survey reference · Outcome · Relief from accountability granted (Y/N) · Incident kit cross-reference.

### Section 12 — Disposition & Disposal — ~8 pp.
*(restored)* Determination date · Reason (excess / obsolete / beyond economical repair / end of useful life / surplus) · Disposition method (transfer / donation / sale / recycling / trade-in / abandonment or destruction) · Authorization document number · Approving official · Disposal date · Receiving entity · Proceeds as entered by the user · Certificate of destruction reference · **Data sanitization performed (Y/N), method, certifying official, date** — required for any data-bearing asset · Final record closure signature and date.

### Section 13 — Photographic Asset Record — ~10 pp.
Provides visual confirmation of asset identity, serial number, location, physical condition, configuration, installed position, damage, and changes. Photo reference number · Asset ID · Date taken · Photographer · Subject · Condition depicted · Notes. Mounting pages with fixed photo wells and caption blocks.

### Section 14 — Supporting Documentation Index — ~6 pp.
Document number · Type (purchase order / invoice / packing slip / receiving report / hand receipt / transfer document / inspection report / certificate of destruction / warranty / photograph / correspondence) · Date · Source · Storage location · Retention period · Disposal-eligible date · Associated asset ID.

### Section 15 — Audit, Review & Certification — ~4 pp.
Review date · Reviewer and organization · Review type (internal / IG / GAO / external audit / change-of-custodian) · Scope · Findings · Corrective action and due date · Closure date · Signature. Periodic certification statement · Accountable property officer signature and date · Supervisor signature and date · Period covered.

### Appendices A–F — ~14 pp.
A. Condition classification reference · B. Accountability classification definitions (accountable / capitalized / sensitive / expendable) · C. Asset category list · D. Disposition pathway glossary · E. Retention schedule worksheet and glossary (NSN, IUID, UII, GFP, APO, LDDT, BER) · F. Blank continuation sheets for §2, §8, §9.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Settled: the Property Documentation Kit leads Stage 2 validation, not this one** (#111, amended). This kit held position 1 before the #113 taxonomy reconciliation, which is why the original gate appeared to name it. **Consequence for this kit:** the restored accountability spine (§4, §6, §7, §9, §12) is the most demanding content in the line and will not be tested first. That is a deliberate trade — the facility kit tests the shared architecture, and spine-specific adoption risk stays open until this kit reaches validation on its own.
2. **The restored spine makes this the most demanding kit to fill in.** That is the point — it is also what makes it defensible. But it is a real adoption cost, and it is the first thing a validating user will push back on. Worth watching for specifically rather than treating pushback as a signal to cut.
3. **§8 Asset Change Log overlaps §7 Transfer and §10 Condition** by design — the log is a chronological summary across all assets, the others are the authoritative records. Same double-entry concern flagged in the superseded maintenance outline; worth testing.
4. **NSN and IUID/UII will be blank for most civilian-agency assets.** They matter to defense-adjacent buyers and read as clutter to everyone else. Consider whether they belong in the register or only in the detail spread.
