## 0. Format decisions

**Shared with the line** (#112, #113): record-spread architecture, boundary language, recordkeeping conventions, regulatory Posture 1, restrained institutional visual direction — Pine / Ink / Paper. Stamp Red is functionally correct throughout this kit and is used more here than anywhere else in the line. Brass Amber dropped.

**Decided for this kit:**

- **Capacity:** 25 incidents per binder. Justification: for most offices 25 incidents represents several years of records, so the binder spans a realistic retention period rather than a reporting cycle.

**Boundary language** — line standard, plus two additions specific to this kit, both non-negotiable:

- **The kit does not assign fault, liability, or culpability.** It records what was observed, when, and by whom. Determination of liability is the agency's, through its own process.
- **The kit does not value a loss or estimate a claim.** The user enters their own figures from their own sources.

**Struck from the supplied copy (#113):** "insurance-related documentation" as a listed use case. It drifts toward WholeClaim's consumer claims-advocacy positioning, which the Product Bible guards against, and reads differently in a government context than it does on the consumer side.

**Scope note:** affected assets are identified here and described in the Asset & Inventory Kit; damaged building systems cross-reference the Property Documentation Kit; repair work is executed and recorded in the Maintenance & Inspection Kit.

## 1. Product definition

**Buyer:** property custodian, safety officer, security officer, or administrative officer responsible for documenting incidents affecting government property.

**Job it does:** start the record immediately and keep it chronological — what happened, what was observed, what was affected, what actions followed — so it remains reviewable later by personnel who were not present.

**Core principle** (front matter): record the condition before the memory changes. A strong incident record establishes a chronological factual history reviewable by people who were not there.

## 2. Section structure

### Front Matter — ~4 pp.
Title page · What this kit is and is not (including both boundary additions) · How to use this kit · **Contemporaneous recording guidance**: record what was observed; distinguish observation from inference; do not speculate on cause; correct by single-line strike with initial and date; never erase or obliterate · Recordkeeping conventions · Core principle.

### Section 1 — Organization & Accountability Profile — ~2 pp.
Agency or department · Sub-office · Facility · Responsible officer (name, title, contact) · Designation reference and date · Alternate · Supervisor of record · Fiscal year · Record period covered · Binder serial and volume.

### Section 2 — Incident Register — ~8 pp.
Landscape index, 4 rows per page. Incident number · Facility · Location · Date of occurrence · Date discovered · Incident type (water intrusion / storm / fire / equipment failure / vandalism / theft / accident / structural / environmental / other) · Brief description · Person reporting · Department · Affected property reference · Estimated value as entered by the user · Operational impact · Status · Detail-spread page reference.

### Sections 3–6 — Per-Incident Record Spread — ~50 pp. (2 pp. × 25)
One two-page opening per incident.

**3. Incident Identification** — Incident number · Facility · Location and space ID · Date and approximate time of occurrence · Date and time discovered · Discovered by (name, title) · Person reporting · Department · Date and time reported · Incident type · Initial description as observed · Persons present · Reporting official signature and date.

**4. Initial Condition & Observation** — Affected areas · Affected assets · Visible damage as observed · Environmental conditions · Weather if relevant · Safety concerns identified · Operational impact · Photograph references · Video references where permitted · Immediate actions taken · Recorded by and time.

**5. Damage Record** — Asset or property item · Asset & Inventory Kit cross-reference · Property Documentation Kit system or space cross-reference · Location at time of incident · Condition before as evidenced by prior records · Damage observed · Damage classification (destroyed / major / minor / missing / undamaged) · Photograph reference · Operational status after · Temporary disposition · Follow-up required · Assessed by and date.

**6. Immediate Actions & Notifications** — Action taken · Date and time · Taken by · Scene secured (Y/N, by whom, time) · Property secured or relocated · Hazard mitigated · Notifications made: recipient, role, method, date and time, acknowledged by · Police or security report number · Higher headquarters notified · Facility management notified.

### Section 7 — Response Log — ~8 pp.
Chronological across the incident. Date and time · Action taken · Person or department · Contractor or vendor · Agency contact · Result · Next action · Recorded by. Designed for entries to be added as events unfold, each independently dated and attributed rather than rewritten into a narrative.

### Section 8 — Recovery Record — ~8 pp.
Structured as the recovery sequence: *incident → initial response → stabilization → repair → restoration → closeout*. Stage · Date started · Date completed · Actions in stage · Responsible party · Contractor or vendor · Areas or assets addressed · Photograph references · Maintenance & Inspection Kit work-order cross-reference · Outstanding items entering the next stage · Stage verification and signature.

### Section 9 — Expense & Supporting Records — ~8 pp.
All figures user-entered with source recorded; the kit computes nothing. Expense category (emergency services / repairs / cleanup / temporary measures / replacement / contractor activity) · Description · Amount · Source of figure (estimate / invoice / purchase record / work order) · Document reference · Date · Vendor or provider · Funding source · Recovered amount (restitution / salvage) · Net cost · Fiscal year · Certifying official.

### Section 10 — Communication Record — ~6 pp.
Date · Sender and recipient · Department · Method (email / letter / phone / in person / portal) · Subject · Summary of content · Related document reference · Action required · Follow-up and date · Recorded by.

### Section 11 — Photographic Documentation — ~12 pp.
The largest photographic section in the line, deliberately. Photo reference number · Incident number · Date and time taken · Photographer · Location and view or angle · Subject depicted · Stage (initial / during response / after repair / final) · Notes. Mounting pages with fixed photo wells and caption blocks beneath.

> The superseded outline carried chain-of-custody fields here. They are removed with the adopted taxonomy — including them implied the kit supports evidentiary handling, which is a commitment the product is not positioned to make.

### Section 12 — Closeout Record — ~6 pp.
Final condition · Repairs completed · Assets replaced · Assets dispositioned (Asset & Inventory Kit cross-reference) · Outstanding issues · Final photograph references · Supporting records index · Completion date · Responsible department · Lessons and follow-up items where appropriate · Closeout certification signature and date.

### Section 13 — Supporting Documentation Index — ~6 pp.
Document number · Type (incident report / police or security report / photograph / invoice / estimate / work order / purchase record / correspondence / inspection report) · Date · Source · Storage location · Retention period · Disposal-eligible date · Associated incident number.

### Section 14 — Audit, Review & Certification — ~4 pp.
Review date · Reviewer and organization · Review type (internal / IG / external audit / administrative review) · Scope · Findings · Corrective action and due date · Closure date · Signature. Periodic certification statement · Responsible officer signature and date · Supervisor signature and date · Period covered.

### Appendices A–F — ~12 pp.
A. Incident type definitions · B. Damage classification definitions · C. Notification worksheet — agency-completed, not prescribed by the kit · D. Recovery stage definitions · E. Retention schedule worksheet and glossary · F. Blank continuation sheets for §2, §7, §11.

## 5. Open questions

1. **The narrowing is a real trade, not just a simplification.** Removing investigation, cause determination, witness statements and liability determination makes the kit cheaper to clear legally and easier to fill in — but it also means the kit stops short of the point where an incident record usually has to go. A user who needs those sections will either improvise them or use something else. Worth confirming with counsel whether the narrowed scope is genuinely lower-risk, or merely smaller.
2. **§8 Recovery Record is the one section with no equivalent in the superseded outline** and comes wholly from the supplied copy. It is a good addition — it gives the kit a spine the original lacked — but it is untested and its stage boundaries are asserted, not derived.
3. **Fault-avoidance holds only if field wording holds.** §4's "visible damage as observed" and §5's damage classification sit closest to the line. Both should record observation and never invite conclusion. This is the specific thing the counsel read should examine.
4. **Prior-condition evidence** (§5) depends on the other kits actually having been maintained. An incident record is far stronger when a Property Documentation or Asset & Inventory binder already exists — that cross-kit dependency is the line's best argument for owning more than one kit, and worth testing as a sales story during Stage 2.
