# A. WHAT THIS KIT IS AND IS NOT

This kit is a documentation and recordkeeping system. It provides a standardized structure for recording the identification, acquisition, custody, location, condition, movement, and disposition of movable government property.

**This kit is not:**

- An official government form. It is not issued by, endorsed by, or affiliated with any agency.
- A compliance certification. Completing this kit does not establish that any statutory, regulatory, or agency requirement has been met.
- A substitute for agency policy, applicable law, or procurement requirements.
- Legal, audit, or accounting advice.

The user is responsible for determining what records their agency requires, what retention applies, and whether this format is acceptable for their purposes.

**Core principle.** Every important physical asset should have an identifiable record. Without consistent documentation, institutional knowledge disappears when personnel change.

---

# B. HOW TO USE THIS KIT

One binder documents up to **25 assets** for one responsible unit. Units holding more than 25 assets use additional binders, numbered by volume, rather than compressing the per-asset record.

> **Why 25 and not more.** The per-asset record spread — a full two-page opening for each asset — is what makes this record defensible when someone reviews it. Scale is handled by adding binders, never by thinning the record. A one-line-per-asset list is an inventory; it is not an accountable property record, and it will not answer the questions a property review actually asks.

Complete Section 1 and Section 2 first. Section 1 identifies who is accountable; Section 2 is the index every later section points back to. Sections 3 through 15 may be completed in any order and are intended to be added to over time rather than finished in a single pass.

Each record entry is dated and attributed to the person who made it. A record without a date and a name is incomplete.

Where a section references another kit in this line, enter the cross-reference identifier if that record exists. If it does not, leave the field blank — do not reconstruct it from memory.

**What belongs in a different kit.** The facility itself and its fixed systems are recorded in the Property Documentation Kit. Maintenance and inspection activity is recorded in the Maintenance & Inspection Kit. Full incident documentation is recorded in the Incident & Loss Documentation Kit. This kit records the *asset* — what it is, who holds it, on whose authority, and where it went.

# C. RECORDKEEPING CONVENTIONS

These conventions apply to every entry in this binder.

| Convention | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Ink** | Permanent ink only. Pencil entries are not a record. |
| **Corrections** | Single horizontal strike through the incorrect entry, leaving it legible. Enter the correction adjacent. Initial and date every correction. |
| **Prohibited** | Erasure, correction fluid, obliteration, removal of pages, or backdating. |
| **Date format** | YYYY-MM-DD throughout. |
| **Attribution** | Every entry carries the printed name and title of the person making it. |
| **Signatures** | Signature blocks require an original signature, printed name, title, and date. |
| **Blank fields** | Enter "N/A" where a field does not apply. A blank field is ambiguous; "N/A" is a record. |
| **Continuation** | Where a section is full, continue on a continuation sheet and mark the original "continued on sheet ___". |
| **Values** | Any cost, value, or proceeds figure is entered by the user from a source document. This kit does not calculate, appraise, or estimate value. |

**Documentation standard.** The purpose of this binder is not to collect paperwork. It is to produce a property record that can be understood by personnel who had no part in creating it.

# APPENDIX A — CONDITION CLASSIFICATION REFERENCE

Select one classification per asset per entry. Condition is recorded as observed on a stated date by a named person — it is a dated observation, not a standing property of the asset.

| Classification | Use when |
|---|---|
| **New** | Not yet placed in service, or in service with no observed wear. |
| **Good** | Fully serviceable. Normal wear consistent with age and use. |

# APPENDIX B — ACCOUNTABILITY CLASSIFICATION DEFINITIONS

These four classifications determine how closely an asset is tracked. **Your agency's own thresholds and definitions govern.** The definitions below are plain-language descriptions of common practice, written so the register can be filled in consistently within one binder — they are not a statement of what any rule requires.

| Classification | Plain-language meaning |
|---|---|
| **Accountable** | The unit is answerable for this item's whereabouts and condition. It is recorded, assigned to a custodian, and reconciled during inventory review. |
| **Capitalized** | The item's acquisition cost met your agency's capitalization threshold and it is carried on the financial records. **Enter the threshold your agency applies below — this kit does not supply one.** |
| **Sensitive** | The item warrants close control regardless of cost — because it is portable and attractive to theft, because it bears data, or because your agency designates it so. |
| **Expendable** | Consumed in use and not individually tracked after issue. Recorded here only if your unit chooses to. |

**Thresholds and designations applied in this binder:**

| Item | Value or rule your agency applies | Source document | Entered by | Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Capitalization threshold | | | | |
| Sensitive-item designation rule | | | | |
| Minimum value for accountable tracking | | | | |

> **An asset may carry more than one classification** — a laptop is commonly both sensitive and accountable, and may or may not be capitalized. Where the register allows one entry, record the classification that drives the tightest control, and record the others in the detail spread.

> **Data-bearing assets.** Any asset that stores data is treated as sensitive in this kit regardless of cost, because its disposal carries an obligation the register must be able to surface. See §12 — data sanitization is a required entry for these items, not an optional one.

| Technology equipment | Computers, laptops, tablets, servers, network hardware, monitors |
| Communications equipment | Radios, phones, repeaters, paging and intercom equipment |

| Specialized equipment | Function-specific equipment — laboratory, medical, evidence, recreation, library |
