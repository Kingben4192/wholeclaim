## 2. Business Documentation Problem — Verified

- **Insurance Information Institute** (iii.org, "Filing a business insurance claim") — identifies inventories, receipts, proof of loss, books and records, and property-damage documentation as required for business insurance claims.
- **FEMA.gov** ("What Will FEMA Want to Know When I Apply for Disaster Assistance?") — identifies pre- and post-loss inventories, photographs, receipts, invoices, and other records as documentation used to establish business losses.
- **Georgia Chamber of Commerce** (gachamber.com, Disaster Resource Center) — recommends organized insurance and documentation records and updated inventory information for claims.

**What this establishes:** a real, externally documented business need for organized property, inventory, and insurance documentation.

**What this does NOT establish:** that businesses lack existing software/processes; that WholeClaim's proposed business offer fills an unserved gap; willingness to pay; pricing; market size; purchase intent. Business commercial validation remains reserved for Step 7 (buyer contact).

## 3. Nonprofit Documentation Problem — Verified, But Materially Narrowed

Fresh competitive verification confirms nonprofits already have access to substantial asset-management systems:

- **MIP** (mip.com) — fixed-asset management for nonprofits: acquisition, depreciation, disposal, warranties, serial/tag info, photos, auditing, reporting.
- **Asset Panda** (assetpanda.com) — nonprofit asset management explicitly marketed with **"audit trail," "chain of custody,"** and **"audit history"** language; covers asset records, depreciation, maintenance, warranties, photos/manuals, insurance info, reporting, inspections, lifecycle tracking.
- **GoCodes** (gocodes.com) — nonprofit asset management covering physical inventory, disposition records, inspections, donated equipment, insurance documentation. *[Note: a claimed "2 CFR 200 compliance" feature is reported but not independently verified — treat as secondary, not load-bearing.]*
- **RedBeam** (redbeam.com) — dedicated nonprofit inventory/asset management: audit, grant, and insurance records; condition, maintenance, funding source, disposal, replacement; explicit asset-compliance-tracking module maintaining "a full audit trail by capturing last known location and assigned custodian." *[Note: a claimed church-specific product extension is reported but not independently verified — treat as secondary, not load-bearing.]*

**What this changes:** the original broad proposition "nonprofits need better property documentation" is not a credible narrow niche by itself. The market already contains products addressing asset records, audit trails, inventory, chain-of-custody-type controls, accounting/fixed assets, compliance, maintenance, warranties, and insurance documentation. This is genuine competitive crowding.

**Framing note:** this strengthens Decision #124's original white-space guardrail rather than undermining it — that guardrail was written against a weaker version of this same finding. The open question narrows to whether a paper/manual structure serves something these software products leave unserved — not whether anyone else is naming the problem.

**Vocabulary caution (relevant to #125's approved-vocabulary list):** "audit trail" and "chain of custody" are accurate, sourced terms and are not banned under #121. But they are now competitor-associated language (Asset Panda, RedBeam use them directly in their own marketing). Using them in WholeClaim copy would put positioning on competitors' ground. Recorded as a positioning caution, not a prohibition.

## 4. Revised Nonprofit Hypothesis

The remaining hypothesis is narrower and more falsifiable:

**Do actual nonprofit audit findings reveal recurring documentation/evidence failures that remain materially distinct from the accounting, fixed-asset, inventory, compliance, and asset-management problems already addressed by existing systems?**

The proposed opportunity cannot be validated merely by showing nonprofits have documentation needs — it must identify a failure that survives despite these adjacent tools existing.

---

## 5. Falsification Test for FAC Research (unchanged from #126)

**Weakened/failed if** FAC findings primarily concern capitalization, depreciation, accounting treatment, inventory controls, custody/control procedures, disposition, grant compliance, or policy implementation already addressed by established systems (Fixed Asset Pro, MIP, Asset Panda, GoCodes, RedBeam).

**More interesting if** findings reveal a distinct evidence/documentation problem — e.g., an organization possessing property but lacking sufficiently organized historical evidence of ownership, acquisition, condition, location, maintenance, damage, or loss needed to substantiate a recovery/consequential event.

Even such a finding would establish a problem, not a market. Crowding changes what a *positive* result is worth — it does not change what the test measures.

## 8. Current Research Conclusion

**Verified:**
- Business property/insurance documentation is a real documented need (Triple-I, FEMA, Georgia Chamber).
- Nonprofits have substantial property/asset/audit/compliance/insurance-documentation needs.
- Existing nonprofit asset-management products (MIP, Asset Panda, GoCodes, RedBeam) address significant portions of this territory, including audit-trail/chain-of-custody language.
- A broad "better nonprofit documentation" proposition is insufficiently differentiated.

**Unverified:**
- Whether nonprofit audit findings reveal a distinct evidence/documentation failure not adequately addressed by existing systems.
- Whether such a failure is sufficiently consequential/recurring to support a dedicated product.
- Whether any organization would pay for the proposed solution.
- GoCodes' 2 CFR 200 compliance claim; RedBeam's church-specific product claim (secondary, not independently confirmed).

- InsureLoc and all conclusions based on it. Confirmed 404 on direct fetch, three independent search attempts found nothing.
