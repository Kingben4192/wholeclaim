## 0. Format decisions

**Shared with the line** (#112, #113): record-spread architecture, boundary language, recordkeeping conventions, regulatory Posture 1, restrained institutional visual direction — Pine / Ink / Paper, Stamp Red reserved for overdue and safety-critical flags, Brass Amber dropped.

**Decided for this kit:**

- **Capacity:** 25 equipment items or systems per binder. Justification: sized for the small-shop, single-facility, and field-site buyer identified in §1 — **not** for a large maintenance organization, which runs hundreds of items and belongs in a CMMS rather than a binder. This is a deliberate narrowing to the buyer the kit can actually serve.

**Boundary language** — the line's standard front matter, plus one addition specific to this kit:

> **The kit does not specify maintenance intervals or establish what maintenance is required.** The user records their own program against manufacturer specifications and agency requirements. Setting intervals would be engineering guidance, which this line does not give.

**Scope note:** the asset itself is described in the Asset & Inventory Kit; the facility and its fixed systems in the Property Documentation Kit. Both are cross-referenced, never duplicated. This kit records *dated events* — inspections performed, work done, deficiencies found and closed — as distinct from the standing condition records held in the Property Documentation Kit.

## 1. Product definition

**Buyer:** maintenance supervisor, shop foreman, building operations officer, or facility manager running inspection and maintenance work at a single site.

**Job it does:** maintain a reliable record of *what was observed → when it was observed → what was done → what remains outstanding*, so that an inspection becomes an organizational record rather than institutional memory.

**Core principle** (front matter): an inspection without a reliable record becomes institutional memory. A documented inspection becomes an organizational record.

## 2. Section structure

### Front Matter — ~4 pp.
Title page · What this kit is and is not (including the interval-setting boundary) · How to use this kit · Recordkeeping conventions · Core principle · Before/after documentation standard: *original condition → work performed → result*.

### Section 1 — Organization & Accountability Profile — ~2 pp.
Agency or department · Sub-office or shop · Facility served · Maintenance supervisor (name, title, contact) · Designation reference and date · Alternate · Supervisor of record · Fiscal year · Record period covered · Binder serial and volume.

### Section 2 — Equipment & System Register — ~8 pp.
Landscape index, 4 rows per page. Equipment or system ID · Nomenclature · Manufacturer · Model · Serial number · Asset & Inventory Kit asset ID cross-reference · Property Documentation Kit system ID cross-reference · Location · In-service date · Criticality (mission-critical / essential / routine) · Recurring maintenance program assigned (Y/N) · Status (in service / down / awaiting parts / retired) · Detail-spread page reference.

### Section 3 — Recurring Maintenance Schedule — ~8 pp.
Program-level view across all equipment. Equipment ID · Task ID · Task description · Interval basis (calendar / runtime hours / cycles / condition / seasonal) · Interval value · Last performed date · Next due date · Assigned to (in-house / vendor) · Estimated labor hours · Required parts reference · Lockout-tagout or safety requirement (Y/N) · Status (current / due / overdue).

**Recurring activity examples** (appendix-listed, user-selected): filter replacement · roof inspection · drainage inspection · equipment servicing · safety-related inspections · exterior inspections · interior inspections · seasonal maintenance · other agency-defined recurring activities.

### Sections 4–7 — Per-Equipment Record Spread — ~50 pp. (2 pp. × 25)
One two-page opening per equipment item or system.

**4. Equipment Identification & Service Specification** — Equipment ID · Nomenclature, manufacturer, model, serial · Cross-references to the asset and property kits · Location · In-service date · Manufacturer service manual reference · Lubricant and consumable specifications · Operating parameters · Criticality rating · Warranty status and expiration · Service contract reference.

**5. Recurring Maintenance Completion Log** — Date performed · Task ID · Interval basis reading at service (date / hours / cycles) · Performed by (name; in-house or vendor) · Tasks completed · Parts consumed · Labor hours · Findings or abnormal conditions · Deficiency raised (Y/N) and reference · Next due · Technician signature · Supervisor verification.

**6. Repair & Corrective Maintenance History** — Date reported · Date started · Date completed · Work order number · Reason for work · Failure or symptom description · Cause determined by the user · Corrective action performed · Parts replaced · Labor hours · Downtime hours · Cost (parts / labor / total) as entered by the user · Performed by (personnel or vendor) · Before and after photograph references · Return-to-service verification and signature.

**7. Parts & Materials Record** — Date · Part number · Description · Quantity · Unit cost · Total cost · Source or vendor · Purchase or stock reference · Installed under (work order or task ID) · Warranty on part · Removed part disposition.

### Section 8 — Facility Inspection Record — ~10 pp.
*(merged in from the superseded facility kit)* Facility · Inspection date · Inspector name and organization · Area inspected · Systems inspected · Inspection type (routine / annual / regulatory / safety / environmental / accessibility / insurance / acceptance) · Observed condition · Overall condition rating · Photograph references · Deficiencies identified · Recommended action · Priority · Follow-up date · Report reference · Next inspection due · Inspector signature.

### Section 9 — System Inspection Record — ~8 pp.
Per-system verification, distinct from servicing. Date · System or equipment ID · System type (HVAC / plumbing / electrical / roofing / fire protection / security / building envelope / drainage / emergency systems / other agency-designated) · Inspection type (safety / regulatory / operational / acceptance / post-repair / calibration) · Inspector name and organization · Standard or procedure referenced by the user · Instrument used and its calibration date · Result (pass / fail / conditional) · Readings or measurements · Deficiencies identified · Next inspection due · Inspector signature.

### Section 10 — Deficiency Tracking & Corrective Action — ~10 pp.
*(merged in from the superseded facility kit)* Deficiency ID · Date identified · Identified by · Source (inspection / occupant report / system failure / audit) · Location or space ID · Associated equipment or system ID · Description · Severity and priority (life safety / urgent / routine / deferred) · Estimated correction cost as entered by the user · Temporary action taken · Permanent action required · Responsible party · Target date · Completion date · Verification and verifier · Supporting photograph references · Status.

### Section 11 — Work Order Log — ~10 pp.
Master chronological log across all equipment. Work order number · Date opened · Priority (emergency / urgent / routine / deferred) · Requested by · Equipment or system ID · Location · Description of work requested · Type (recurring maintenance / corrective / modification / inspection) · Assigned to · Date scheduled · Date completed · Labor hours · Total cost · Related deficiency ID · Status · Closed by and date.

### Section 12 — Vendor & Service Provider Register — ~6 pp.
Vendor name · Vendor identifier (UEI or agency-assigned) · Services provided · Contract, BPA, or purchase vehicle number · Contract period · Point of contact (name, phone, email) · After-hours contact · Response-time commitment · Rate structure · Insurance and bonding status with expiration · Equipment or systems covered · Performance notes · Active status.

### Section 13 — Before/After Photographic Record — ~10 pp.
Photo reference number · Equipment or system ID · Date · Photographer · Stage (before / during / after) · Subject (condition / failure / part / nameplate / completed work) · Associated work order or deficiency ID · Notes. Mounting pages arranged as before/after pairs with caption blocks beneath.

### Section 14 — Cost & Supporting Documentation — ~6 pp.
Period (month or quarter) · Equipment ID or cost center · In-house labor hours and cost · Contract labor cost · Parts cost · Materials cost · Total maintenance cost · Funding source · Budget line · Cumulative fiscal-year total. Supporting document index: document number · type (service manual / procedure / work order / invoice / parts receipt / test or calibration certificate / warranty / vendor contract / photograph / correspondence) · date · source · storage location · retention period · disposal-eligible date · associated equipment ID.

### Section 15 — Facility History Summary — ~4 pp.
Built from accumulated records; a periodic roll-up rather than a live log. Repeated problems · Major repairs · Replacement cycles observed · Recurring deficiencies · Significant improvements · Maintenance patterns · Outstanding issues · Period covered · Compiled by and date.

### Section 16 — Audit, Review & Certification — ~4 pp.
Review date · Reviewer and organization · Review type (internal / IG / external audit / warranty / insurance) · Scope · Findings · Corrective action and due date · Closure date · Signature. Periodic certification statement · Maintenance supervisor signature and date · Supervisor signature and date · Period covered.

### Appendices A–F — ~14 pp.
A. Priority and criticality definitions · B. Interval basis reference · C. Recurring activity list · D. Failure cause category list · E. Retention schedule worksheet and glossary · F. Blank continuation sheets for §2, §3, §10, §11.

## 5. Open questions

1. **This kit is now the largest in the line at ~154 pp.**, because the taxonomy merge moved facility inspection and deficiency tracking into it on top of its existing maintenance content. That is a real consequence of the reconciliation and worth a deliberate look: either accept a heavier binder, drop capacity from 25 equipment items to 15, or split inspection back out as its own kit. I have not chosen — the merge was directed by the adopted taxonomy.
2. **This is the kit most likely to lose to software.** CMMS and EAM systems own this workflow in any agency large enough to have one. The paper buyer is the small shop, the field site, or the environment where a system exists but is not trusted. That buyer is real but narrow, and this is the kit whose buyer most needs confirming during Stage 2.
3. **§11 Work Order Log duplicates data** already in §5, §6 and §10 by design — the log is chronological across equipment, the spreads are per-equipment. Defensible on paper, resented in practice. Worth testing directly.
4. **Interval-setting boundary** — confirm the front-matter language holds. Recording maintenance is safe; specifying required maintenance is engineering guidance.
