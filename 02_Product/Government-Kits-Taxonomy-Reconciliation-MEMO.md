## 1. The core problem: Kits 1 and 2 are swapped

The Decision Log names **Kit 1 = Government Property & Asset Record Kit** and makes it the Stage 2 validation basis (#111, #112). In the new copy, **Kit 1 is a facility documentation kit** — which is the outline line's **Kit 2**.

This is not a naming difference. If the copy is adopted silently, "Kit 1" in Decisions #110, #111, and #112 points at a different product than "Kit 1" in the product copy, and the Stage 2 validation gate now names something the log never approved. **Kit numbering is demonstrably unstable and should stop being load-bearing.** Every gate should re-anchor to a kit's name, not its number.

## 3. What the copy's Kit 2 drops from the outline's Kit 1

The outline's asset kit was built around an accountability chain: **acquisition → receipt → custody → transfer → inventory reconciliation → disposition.** The copy's asset kit keeps identification, location, condition, and a change log, and drops:

- National Stock Number (NSN) and IUID/UII item-unique identification
- Acquisition & receipt record (contract/PO reference, acceptance, funding source, capitalization threshold)
- Custody & hand receipt, including the acknowledgment-of-responsibility signature
- Transfer authorization (authorizing document, approving official, receiving acknowledgment)
- Physical inventory reconciliation as a certified process with discrepancy typing
- Loss/damage/destruction/theft and relief-from-accountability
- **Disposition & disposal — including data sanitization certification for data-bearing assets**

**This is the difference between an inventory list and an accountable property record.** An inventory list answers "what do we have." An accountable property record answers "who was responsible, on what authority, and where did it go" — which is what a property review actually asks. The dropped items are also the fields a government buyer would recognize as the ones their own process requires.

Two of these deserve individual attention:

- **Data sanitization on disposal** is the single highest-consequence field in the whole line. A government IT asset leaving custody without a sanitization record is a data-breach exposure. I would not ship an asset kit without it regardless of which taxonomy wins.
- **Custody signature** is what makes the record evidentiary rather than administrative. Without it the kit documents where things are, not who answers for them.

**Assessment:** the copy's asset kit is a materially simpler and more approachable product. That is a legitimate positioning choice — simpler may sell better and is easier to fill in. But it should be chosen deliberately, not inherited by omission.

## 4. Copy Kit 5 is a different product, not a restructure

| | Outline Kit 5 | Copy Kit 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Contracting officer's representative (COR) | Facility or capital project manager |
| Unit | One contract or task order | One construction/renovation project |
| Spine | Contract administration | Before → during → after condition record |
| Carries | Modifications, invoices & payment, approvals, compliance tracking, closeout release of claims, deobligation | Pre-project condition, progress documentation, change documentation, completion, closeout |

The copy's version drops contract administration entirely — no invoice/payment record, no modification instrument tracking, no compliance requirement tracking, no release of claims or deobligation.

**Assessment:** the copy's version is closer to the rest of the line (it is a *condition* record, like Kits 1–4) and avoids the problem I flagged in the outline — that Posture 1's no-citations rule cost the most in a contract-administration kit, and that agency contract writing systems already own that workflow. **The copy's Kit 5 is probably the better product.** This is the one place I think the copy clearly improves on the outline.

## 5. What the copy drops that I would fight for

- **Emergency information & life safety** (outline Kit 2 §12) — utility shutoffs, alarm panel and riser locations, generator, evacuation points, drill records, hazmat locations. Flagged in the original outline as possibly the strongest standalone value in the line: the section most likely to be used daily rather than filed. Absent from the copy entirely.
- **Closeout record** (outline Kit 5 §13) — partially survives in the copy's Kit 5 closeout fields, but loses the financial and contractual completion items.
- **Warranty tracking** (outline Kit 2 §7) — survives only as a closeout line item in the copy, not as a maintained per-system record. Warranty expiry is a recurring, real, money-losing gap in facility management.

## 6. Effect on the Kit 4 legal gate

Decision #112 gates the incident kit's content on counsel review. Two observations:

1. The gate must be **re-anchored to the kit's name**, not "Kit 4," given numbering is now unstable.
2. The copy's incident kit drops exactly the sections that drove the gate — chain of custody, witness statements, investigation and cause determination, financial liability determination. **If the copy's narrower scope is adopted, the counsel gate becomes materially cheaper**, possibly reducible to a boundary-language review rather than a full content review. That is a legitimate way to resolve the risk: remove the risky sections rather than lawyer them. It should be an explicit choice, not a silent consequence.

The copy also lists **"insurance-related documentation"** among the incident kit's use cases. Flagging that directly: it is the one line in the document that drifts toward WholeClaim's consumer insurance-claim positioning, and in a government context it moves toward the claims-advocacy boundary the Product Bible guards. Recommend striking it regardless of which taxonomy governs.

## 7. Layer question — the copy's own note is correct

The copy is **descriptive**: section names and comma-separated field lists, no row layouts, no page counts, no capacity, no signature blocks. The outlines are **structural**: field-by-field, page-budgeted, capacity-decided.

These are different layers and mostly complementary. Adopting the copy's taxonomy does **not** mean discarding the outlines' structure — it means re-cutting that structure along new boundaries. Roughly 70% of the outline field work survives any of the options below; what changes is which binder each section lives in.

The copy is not usable as kit content as written. It is usable as sales-page copy, or as the naming and positioning layer above the structural outlines.

**Register note:** the copy reads warmer and more marketing-flavored ("Know what you own. Know what condition it is in.") than the institutional register the original brief specified. As product-page copy that is defensible. As front matter inside an audit-facing binder it is off-register and should be rewritten.

## 8. Options

**Option A — Copy governs.** Renumber and re-cut the outlines to the copy's five kits. Accept the dropped accountability spine and the simpler asset kit. Fastest path to a coherent line; loses the audit-defensibility argument that distinguishes this from a generic inventory template.

**Option B — Outlines govern.** Treat the copy as marketing description, rewrite it against the existing structure. Preserves depth; but keeps the contract-administration Kit 5 that I already flagged as the weakest product in the line, and discards a taxonomy that is in several respects better.

**Option C — Merge (recommended).** Adopt the copy's **taxonomy, naming, and positioning** — including its Kit 5, which is the better product — and keep the outlines' **structural depth**, restoring specific dropped sections by explicit decision rather than by default. Specifically: restore the accountability spine (custody signature, transfer authorization, disposition with data sanitization) into the asset kit, and restore emergency/life-safety into the facility kit. Everything else in the copy's simplification stands.

Option C treats the two documents as what they actually are — a positioning layer and a structural layer that disagree about boundaries — rather than as rival drafts.
